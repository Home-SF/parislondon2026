Paris Activities

Sights, museums, and things to do in Paris — with address, hours, and entrance fee for each.

Eiffel Tower

Champ de Mars, 5 Avenue Anatole France, 75007 Paris, France
Mid-June–early Sept: daily 9:00am–12:45am (last ascent ~midnight) · Rest of year: daily 9:30am–11:45pm
€14.80–€36.70 (adult, varies by floor/stairs vs lift)
Did you know? Built for the 1889 World's Fair as a temporary structure meant to come down after 20 years — and it grows about 6 inches taller in summer as the iron expands in the heat.
Well known One of the world's most visited paid monuments, attracting around 7 million visitors a year. Designed by Gustave Eiffel, it was completed in 1889 for the World's Fair.

Musée d'Orsay

1 Rue de la Légion d'Honneur, 75007 Paris, France
Tue–Sun 9:30am–6:00pm (Thu until 9:45pm) · Closed Monday
€16 online / €14 on-site · Free under 18 & EU residents 18–25 · Free first Sunday of the month
Did you know? The museum is housed inside a former Beaux-Arts railway station, Gare d'Orsay, built for the 1900 World's Fair — the giant clock is one of the most photographed spots in Paris.
Well known Housed in the former Gare d'Orsay railway station, it holds the world's largest collection of Impressionist and Post-Impressionist masterpieces, including works by Monet, Renoir, and Van Gogh.

Versailles

Place d'Armes, 78000 Versailles, France
Palace: 9:00am–6:30pm · Estate of Trianon: 12:00pm–6:30pm · Gardens: 7:00am–8:30pm · Park: 7:00am–8:30pm · Palace & Trianon closed Mondays and Dec 25, Jan 1, May 1
Light of Liberty: A 15-min VR experience marking the 250th anniversary of American Independence — meet Lafayette, Franklin & Washington and explore naval battles and diplomacy tied to the Revolution's connection with Versailles. Includes skip-the-line Palace access right after, plus the Estate of Trianon, Gardens, and temporary exhibitions.
Audio guides: 2 rented for the visit.
Palace ~€21 · Passport ticket (Palace + gardens + Trianon) ~€24–32 · Gardens free most days
Did you know? The Hall of Mirrors contains 357 mirrors. Mirrors were once so expensive that filling a room with them was itself a display of royal wealth and power.
Well known The Palace of Versailles was the principal royal residence of France from 1682 until the Revolution. At its peak it housed up to 20,000 people — courtiers, servants, and government officials — all under one roof.

Orangerie — Water Lilies by Monet (whole collection)

Jardin des Tuileries, Place de la Concorde side, 75001 Paris, France
Mon, Wed–Sun 9:00am–6:00pm · Closed Tuesday
€12.50 online / €11 on-site · Free under 18 & EU residents 18–25 · Free first Sunday of the month
Did you know? Monet designed the oval Water Lilies rooms to his own specifications and donated the paintings to France the day after WWI ended, as a monument to peace.
Well known The Musée de l'Orangerie is famous above all for Monet's eight monumental Water Lilies murals, installed in two purpose-built oval rooms the artist helped design before his death in 1926.
Well known Originally laid out for Catherine de' Medici in 1564 as the garden of the Tuileries Palace, the gardens became one of the first public green spaces in Paris after the Revolution.

Well known Commissioned by Napoleon in 1806 to honour his armies, the Arc de Triomphe stands at the centre of the world's largest roundabout and shelters the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier beneath its vault.

Claude Monet's House and Gardens — Giverny

84 Rue Claude Monet, 27620 Giverny, France
Mar 22–Nov 1: Tue–Sun 9:30am–6:00pm · Closed Monday · Closed Nov–Mar · Timed-entry tickets required
€12.50 adult · €7.50 reduced · Free under 7
Day trip from Paris Monet designed this water-lily garden — the Japanese bridge, weeping willows, wisteria — specifically as subjects to paint, spending 43 years altering it. Train from Paris Saint-Lazare to Vernon (~1h 15m), then shuttle or taxi 5 km to Giverny.
Well known Claude Monet lived and painted at Giverny from 1883 until his death in 1926, creating the famous water garden that directly inspired his celebrated Water Lilies series.

Château de Fontainebleau

77300 Fontainebleau, France
Palace: Wed–Mon 9:30am–6:00pm (5:00pm Oct–Mar) · Closed Tuesday, Jan 1, May 1, Dec 25 · Gardens free, open daily
€14 adult · Free under 26 (EU residents) · Paris Muséum Pass accepted
Day trip from Paris Every French monarch from the 12th century to Napoleon III stayed here — the only royal palace used continuously for 800 years. Napoleon called it “the true home of kings.” Transilien R train from Gare de Lyon to Fontainebleau-Avon takes about 40 minutes.
Well known One of the largest French royal châteaux, Fontainebleau has been continuously used by French heads of state for nearly 700 years — from Louis VII to Napoleon III and beyond.

Free
Giverny day-trip stop Three medieval timber mill buildings perched on bridge piers over the Seine — one of Normandy’s most photographed views, and a favourite Monet subject. Vernon is the train station (from Paris Saint-Lazare) on the way to Giverny.
Well known The photogenic mill house balances on the original stone piers of a 13th-century bridge over the Seine — the bridge itself was destroyed centuries ago, leaving the structure stranded mid-river.
