Afternoon walk — Rue Saint-Honoré to Stohrer via Les Halles
Party of 3 · 90 min · Rue Saint-Honoré east → left at Rue du Pont Neuf → Allée André Breton through Les Halles gardens → Rue Montorgueil north to Stohrer

10 things to look out for, in order:
1. La Samaritaine — Approaching Rue du Pont Neuf, look right: the LVMH-restored Art Nouveau landmark, closed 15 years and reopened 2021
2. Bourse de Commerce — Pinault Collection — The round 16th-century grain exchange dome visible off Rue de Viarmes, now a contemporary art museum
3. Fontaine des Innocents (Place Joachim-du-Bellay) — As you enter Les Halles: Paris's oldest surviving monumental fountain, carved in 1549
4. Forum des Halles Canopy — The sweeping glass-and-steel roof over the subterranean complex; peer down into it as you cross above
5. Jardin Nelson-Mandela — The garden above the Forum where Allée André Breton runs; lively square with locals at lunch
6. Église Saint-Eustache — The north face rises dramatically off the garden: Flamboyant Gothic exterior with Renaissance interior, one of the largest churches in Paris
7. Rue Montorgueil market stalls — The pedestrian street opens with fruit vendors, cheese shops, and flower sellers; watch for La Fermette (cheese) and Palais du Fruit
8. L'Escargot Montorgueil (#38) — Look up for the giant gilded snail above the door; restaurant founded 1832, still serves escargot, foie gras and oysters
9. Fou de Pâtisserie (#45) — Paris's first pastry concept shop: rotating creations from the city's top pastry chefs all in one window
10. Stohrer (#51) — The oldest patisserie in Paris, founded 1730 by Louis XV's personal pastry chef; famous for its baba au rhum and the original Art Nouveau painted ceiling inside
